# Compact concepts and distinctions

| Term | Working meaning | Do not confuse with |
|---|---|---|
| Dignity | Inherent respect owed to each person | A reward for contribution or behavior |
| Harm | Material impairment to health, safety, needs, agency, opportunity, or flourishing, assessed in context | Offense, disagreement, criticism, or loss of status alone |
| Preventable harm | Harm plausibly avoidable given responsibility, capacity, knowledge, feasibility, and proportionate cost | Unlimited duty to intervene or blame for outcomes beyond control |
| Fairness | Defensible treatment and distribution attentive to rights, needs, opportunity, burden, evidence, and time | A single number, identical outcomes, or aggregate utility alone |
| Sufficiency | Reliable access to conditions needed for survival, dignity, and meaningful participation | A complete measure of well-being or an objective universal threshold |
| Contribution | Contextual beneficial activity, often collective or difficult to measure | Human worth, moral character, or entitlement to basic needs |
| Power | Capacity to shape choices or outcomes, including through wealth, information, infrastructure, or authority | Wealth alone or a single scalar |
| Stability | Resilience of systems that support people and living beings | Preservation of incumbents or suppression of dissent |
| Justice | Rights-respecting prevention, accountability, repair, and protection | Revenge, prediction alone, or optimization without due process |
| AI role | Support for analysis, explanation, and scenario exploration | Legitimate authority, moral agency, or autonomous governance |
| Consent | Informed, meaningful participation with practical recourse | A checkbox, coerced agreement, or loss of essentials for opting out |

## Important constraints

- A score describes a defined measurement task; it does not describe the whole person.
- Correlation and prediction do not establish causation, intent, or individual guilt.
- Aggregate benefits do not automatically justify severe or unevenly distributed harms.
- A threshold or weight encodes value judgments even if expressed mathematically.
- More agents do not guarantee independent evidence or safer conclusions.
- "Auditable" requires real access, competent independent scrutiny, and a remedy for errors.
- Any coercive decision requires legitimate human authority and due process; a model cannot supply either.
