# Three-pass alignment review

## Review protocol

This file records three conceptual rechecks of each proposed algorithm. In every pass the questions are explicitly asked and answered:

1. **Does algorithm X match Foundation principle Y?**
2. **Does algorithm X fit with the original manifesto?**

`YES` means the *written design as bounded here* appears consistent on review; it does not mean empirically validated, legally approved, or safe to deploy. `NO` would require revision and a fresh check. The manifesto questions refer especially to moral equivalence, non-harm through action and inaction, power that does not accumulate unchecked, dignity/basic needs, non-retributive justice, consent, and AI as tool rather than authority.

For every algorithm, the improvement branch is also shown. Can this be improved upon? **YES**—designs can always be improved. Is the further improvement required to meet the initial conceptual goal? **NO, after the safeguards and corrections recorded here**: the minimum concept meets its limited goal of human-reviewed analysis, not real-world deployment. Therefore ask the final hypothetical question. The answer **YES, conditionally** means it could contribute to human benefit if legitimate, rights-respecting institutions adopt it, evidence supports it, affected people participate, and safeguards work. It is not a prediction or a claim that the algorithm as written has demonstrated benefit. Any such improvements remain a reason to revise and re-audit before implementation.

A reviewer should use `NO` if the answer is not supported. Never change a `NO` to `YES` to force approval; revise and ask again.
