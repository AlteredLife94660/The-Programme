# Scope and shared safeguards

## Status and scope

The algorithms here are conceptual workflows for research, deliberation, and low-stakes assistance. They should be tested in simulations and reviewed with affected communities before any real-world use. No performance claim is made. The manifesto's values are treated as aims and constraints, not evidence that a specific algorithm is correct.

## Common input discipline

For each request, record only information necessary for the stated purpose, with lawful basis and privacy protections. Separate:

- verified observations and their sources;
- statements by affected people, including disagreement;
- estimates, model outputs, and uncertainty;
- assumptions, value choices, and unresolved decisions.

Do not infer intent, character, identity-based worth, or guilt from a proxy or group statistic. Do not treat missing data as evidence against a person. Keep sensitive personal data out of model prompts and records unless specifically necessary, lawful, consented to where required, and protected.

## Common stop conditions

Pause automated analysis and refer to a qualified, accountable human process if:

- a decision could restrict rights, liberty, essential needs, movement, speech, association, or access to a service;
- there is credible imminent danger, a safeguarding duty, or a need for emergency services;
- evidence is contested, unreliable, insufficient, or materially incomplete;
- a model's result conflicts with an affected person's account or lacks an accessible explanation;
- impacts may be severe, irreversible, discriminatory, or concentrated on a vulnerable group;
- the request would surveil people, suppress peaceful dissent, assign a human-worth score, or evade legal process.

The human process must have legitimate authority, appropriate expertise, notice, reasons, independent review, appeal, and remedy. This document does not define or replace applicable emergency procedures or law.

## Shared sequence

1. **Clarify purpose:** State the question and what decisions are out of scope.
2. **Check legitimacy:** Identify the accountable human decision-maker, affected parties, lawful basis, and consent/participation requirements.
3. **Gather proportionate evidence:** Minimize data; preserve provenance and uncertainty.
4. **Consider alternatives:** Include non-AI and non-coercive options, and the option of taking no action.
5. **Assess impacts:** Examine rights, distribution, individual and group effects, short- and long-term consequences, and reversibility.
6. **Generate options, not commands:** Explain trade-offs and limits without presenting a score as truth.
7. **Human decision and recourse:** A legitimate human authority decides consequential matters; provide understandable reasons and ways to contest and correct.
8. **Monitor and retire:** Independently evaluate outcomes, publish appropriate findings, set review/sunset dates, and stop use if safeguards fail.

## Non-negotiable invariants

- Human dignity and basic needs are independent of contribution, predicted behavior, or compliance.
- No single agent may govern, audit itself, or expand its own authority.
- AI output never creates legal or democratic authority.
- Fairness is not a single scalar; no weighted score overrides rights or a serious harm floor.
- Peaceful criticism, protest, journalism, and disagreement are not themselves instability or harm.
- Any coercive measure requires lawful human authority and due process; these algorithms cannot initiate one.
- Inaction analysis is bounded by an actor's role, knowledge, capacity, feasible options, and proportionate cost.
- Use the least restrictive effective response, time-limit it, and independently review it.
- Maintain a safe shutdown, rollback, and continuity plan for any future technical implementation.
