# Open questions and decisions reserved for people

These questions are intentionally unresolved. An AI must not silently choose answers, invent consensus, or convert an illustrative value into policy. Record who decides, whose views are represented, the evidence used, dissenting views, and how a decision can be revisited.

## Moral scope and conflicts

- What forms of moral consideration are owed to humans, animals, ecosystems, and future generations, and how should conflicts be addressed?
- What does equal consideration require when needs, vulnerability, agency, and effects differ?
- Which rights are non-negotiable, and what legitimate process may define or amend them?

## Fairness and measurement

- Which dimensions of fairness matter in a specific context, and who participates in choosing them?
- How should historical disadvantage, present need, individual rights, and aggregate outcomes be balanced?
- What evidence can support measures of harm, opportunity, stability, contribution, or power?
- How should uncertainty, missing data, conflicting testimony, and model error affect action?
- Which outcomes must never be traded away for a higher aggregate score?

## Sufficiency and scarcity

- What services and material conditions are essential in different places and circumstances?
- How should genuine scarcity be managed without discrimination, coercion, or avoidable deprivation?
- What mix of income, public services, infrastructure, and voluntary support best protects autonomy and dignity?
- What due process is required before any compulsory allocation or limitation on use?

## Authority and accountability

- Which decisions, if any, may AI support, and which must remain entirely with accountable human institutions?
- What constitutes independent oversight when systems, vendors, or data sources are interconnected?
- How can a person obtain understandable reasons, correct records, appeal, and receive a remedy?
- What data collection is necessary, who controls it, and when must it be deleted?
- What reliable safeguards exist for shutdown, replacement, rollback, and continuity of essential services?

## Power and contribution

- How can entrenched power be reduced without disrupting legitimate security, collective projects, or basic rights?
- What forms of care, maintenance, learning, and community work are overlooked by conventional indicators?
- Should any contribution-based recognition exist, and what must it never affect?
- How can policies avoid turning temporary recognition into permanent hierarchy?

## Legitimacy and change

- How can participation be genuinely voluntary when institutions and services are interdependent?
- What processes ensure that affected and marginalized communities have meaningful influence?
- How are dissent, peaceful opposition, and minority rights protected?
- What conditions trigger policy review, suspension, or peaceful replacement?

## Decision record template

For each major proposal, document:

1. Decision and scope.
2. Values and rights implicated.
3. People and communities affected, including those absent from the data.
4. Options considered, including doing nothing and non-AI alternatives.
5. Evidence, assumptions, uncertainties, and dissent.
6. Expected benefits, harms, distributional effects, and reversibility.
7. Legal basis, accountable decision-maker, and oversight.
8. Notice, appeal, remedy, and privacy protections.
9. Evaluation plan, review date, and sunset or rollback conditions.
