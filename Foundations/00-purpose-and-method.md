# Purpose and method

## Why this framework exists

The programme explores whether institutions and, eventually, multi-agent AI systems can help people coordinate toward a more humane and sustainable society. Its motivating aims are to reduce preventable harm, ensure that people can meet basic needs, distribute opportunity fairly, sustain creativity, and prevent durable concentrations of power.

These aims are aspirations for inquiry, not proof that one formula or institutional design can deliver them. The work should remain open to evidence, criticism, revision, and peaceful rejection.

## Humanistic starting point

People are not optimization targets. Each person has dignity, agency, relationships, rights, and a perspective that cannot be fully represented by a score. A framework intended to benefit humanity must protect people from both direct harm and harm done in the name of fairness, safety, efficiency, or stability.

The source texts extend moral consideration to non-human life and future generations. This foundation retains that concern while recognizing that the scope and practical meaning of moral consideration remain open questions. Equal consideration does not automatically imply identical treatment in every circumstance; differences must be explained, evidence-based, proportionate, and reviewable.

## How to read the source material

`Sources/` contains the original proposals, including example equations and code. Preserve them as historical source material. Do not treat illustrative weights, thresholds, sample Python, or categorical claims as empirically established or ready for deployment.

`Foundations/` reformats and expands the ideas, distinguishing:

- **Values:** what the programme hopes to protect or promote.
- **Principles:** constraints that should guide deliberation.
- **Hypotheses:** claims about what policies or mechanisms may work.
- **Measures:** possible indicators that require validation and may be incomplete.
- **Decisions:** choices that require legitimate, accountable human processes.

`Initial/` is a compact orientation for language models. It is not a higher authority and must not be used to bypass the safeguards in this directory.

## Method commitments

1. State uncertainty rather than inventing certainty.
2. Assess effects on different people and groups, including those with less power.
3. Distinguish observed outcomes from inferred intent, character, or worth.
4. Prefer the least restrictive effective option and preserve basic rights.
5. Make significant decisions explainable, appealable, and reversible where possible.
6. Treat affected people as participants, not merely data sources.
7. Test for disparate impacts, manipulation, gaming, and unintended consequences.
8. Revisit policies on a published schedule and when evidence or circumstances change.
9. Keep an auditable record of assumptions, uncertainty, dissent, and reasons.
10. Never present an AI-generated recommendation as a legitimate decision merely because it is consistent or numerically precise.

## Non-goals

This work does not claim that fairness can be reduced to one score, that human judgment can or should be removed, that all disagreement is measurable, or that a model can define society's values on behalf of its members. It does not authorize surveillance, coercion, deprivation, censorship, detention, or restrictions on rights. Those powers require legitimate law, due process, and accountable institutions; an AI system cannot grant itself such authority.
