# Justice and governance

## Justice as prevention, repair, and rights

`Sources/Algo-Justice` reframes justice as reducing future harm rather than inflicting revenge. This is a valuable aspiration, but risk reduction alone is insufficient: justice also requires rights, fair process, accurate fact-finding, accountability, support for affected people, and protection from arbitrary power.

Past allegations, model predictions, or group-level statistics must not be treated as proof that an individual will cause harm. No AI-generated risk score may by itself justify surveillance, denial of essential services, restriction of movement, relocation, isolation, or other coercive action.

## A humane response sequence

When a credible concern arises, institutions should first clarify immediate safety needs and the affected people's wishes, preserve evidence lawfully, and distinguish verified facts from uncertainty. Consider, in order as appropriate: voluntary support and prevention; information and accessible services; restorative or reparative options chosen without coercion; proportionate lawful restrictions where necessary; and emergency protection under applicable law.

This is not a rigid ladder. The response must fit the situation, protect those at risk, and respect the rights of everyone involved. Any restriction must have a legitimate legal basis, be necessary and proportionate, use the least restrictive effective means, have a defined duration, and be independently reviewed. Detention or isolation requires especially strong safeguards, representation, humane conditions, and prompt appeal.

## Governance of AI-supported systems

The proposed multi-agent architecture can distribute analytical work, but multiple agents do not automatically create independence or safety. Agents can share data, assumptions, failure modes, and incentives. An auditor model is not independent merely because it has a different name.

For any consequential AI use:

- define a narrow, publicly authorized purpose and prohibit unrelated use;
- keep accountable human institutions responsible for decisions;
- provide meaningful notice, reasons, appeal, and remedy to affected people;
- document model versions, data, limitations, and decision pathways;
- conduct independent security, privacy, fairness, and impact evaluations;
- test failure, manipulation, correlated-agent errors, and safe shutdown;
- allow external oversight and participation by affected communities;
- ensure services continue if a model fails, is contested, or is withdrawn.

Transparency must be balanced with privacy and safety; it does not mean publishing sensitive personal data. Auditability requires access by legitimate independent reviewers and clear records, not indiscriminate public exposure.

## Contestability and consent

People need accessible ways to challenge information, correct errors, understand decisions, and seek human review without retaliation or loss of basic services. Consent must be informed and revocable where appropriate. Where a system is essential to public life, individual opt-out alone may not resolve collective legitimacy; provide meaningful democratic participation, alternatives, and a peaceful means to reform or replace the system.

## Limits on stability claims

"Stability" is not a blank check. Criticism, protest, journalism, minority beliefs, and peaceful organizing are essential to accountability and should not be classified as destabilization merely because they challenge institutions. Responses to demonstrable coordinated harm must target the harmful conduct with evidence and due process, not suppress viewpoints or opposition.
