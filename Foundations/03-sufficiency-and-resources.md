# Sufficiency and resources

## Aim

The resource proposals in `Sources/Algo-Resources` and `Sources/Manifesto-v1` express a humane aspiration: no person's survival or dignity should depend on proving economic productivity. This document develops that aspiration without treating the source equations as a deployable allocation policy.

## Basic-needs commitment

A just system should work toward dependable, dignified access to essentials, including nutritious food, safe shelter, healthcare, water and sanitation, energy where needed, personal safety, communication, and the practical ability to participate in community life. The exact bundle and standard will vary with circumstances, disability, climate, culture, local infrastructure, and available evidence. Affected communities should help define it.

Access should not be conditioned on a contribution score or on labor. The framework should distinguish inability, unwillingness, need, preference, and scarcity rather than infer one from another. In emergencies, prioritize preventing severe and irreversible deprivation.

## Sufficiency is not a score of a person

The source model proposes resource vectors and minimum/sufficiency thresholds. Such thresholds can help identify gaps, but they are not objective simply because they are numeric. They may miss quality, stability, accessibility, safety, personal circumstances, and the person's own choices. Use them as indicators for offering support and investigating unmet needs—not as definitive judgments about eligibility or worth.

A person's surplus in one category does not automatically compensate for deprivation in another. Access to an additional resource also may not be fungible: money cannot always replace healthcare, safety, housing, or autonomy.

## Allocation when resources are scarce

The principle that preventable severe need should be addressed before discretionary surplus is a strong starting point, not an instruction for an AI to seize resources. Allocation under genuine scarcity requires legitimate, transparent rules; evidence about urgency and effectiveness; safeguards against discrimination; human review; and ways to appeal. The rules should explain how they account for competing urgent needs, feasibility, rights, and unequal starting conditions.

Prefer voluntary sharing, public provisioning, access improvements, and incentives where effective. Any compulsory measure would require lawful authority, necessity, proportionality, due process, and independent oversight. Automated detection of "hoarding" or forced redistribution is not authorized by these documents.

## Income, services, and choice

The source texts mention basic income and transfers. These are policy options, not conclusions compelled by a formula. Cash, services, public infrastructure, and targeted assistance have different benefits and risks. Evaluate accessibility, autonomy, administrative burden, privacy, adequacy, local conditions, and unintended effects. Provide meaningful choice where feasible and avoid intrusive collection of personal data.

## Resilience and environmental limits

Resource security also depends on resilient systems and sustainable use. Plan for shocks and long-term ecological effects without using speculative forecasts to strip people of present rights. Assess who bears transition costs, protect those most exposed, and revisit assumptions as conditions change.
